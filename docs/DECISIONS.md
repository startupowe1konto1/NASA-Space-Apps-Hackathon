- D42 (Sat 18:20, user via O1, #105 item 1): How is drought counted? → Against the same calendar month: a month is a drought when its root-zone wetness is below the 20th percentile of that month over the field's record (July against every July). Replaces D30. Drought indices compare a month this way (WMO SPI User Guide, WMO-No. 1090, p. 13; U.S. Drought Monitor percentiles), and with D30 a spring month could almost never be a drought (Poznań rapeseed: none in 45 years). Accepted trade-off: every month is a drought in at most 1 year in 5 at every field (fewer where POWER's two-decimal wetness values tie at the limit, because a tie is not below it: 11–20% per month at the Iowa field), so drought separates crops mostly by how many months they need water, not by field. The calendar marks drought in every sensitive month with a drought year, whatever `markShare` is; heat and frost keep `markShare`. The field summary drops "highest drought month" and counts drought years in the last 15 seasons.
- D43 (Sat 18:20, user via O1, #105 item 2): How is heat counted? → From NASA POWER daily `T2M_MAX`: a month is a heat month for a crop when at least 3 days are above the crop's threshold (`hazards.heat.minDays`, team rule backed by FAO-66 barley "even only 3 days" and the pea source's "a few days"). Monthly `T2M_MAX` is the hottest single hour, so one hot afternoon counted as a heat month. The 3 days are counted inside the calendar month and need not be consecutive; a spell across two months may count in neither. Crop thresholds stay. A profile without daily values (fixtures) falls back to the monthly maximum. Income still calls the heat check with one day until O3 passes `heatMinDays` after #100.
